Nothing was cut or glued to make the picture below. It came from the prompt "Simplified architectural 3D model of the Criciúma viewpoint tower".

Architecture is where paper behaves best. Straight folds, square corners, almost no shaping required.

Low poly means the shape is described by a small number of planes. Folded paper does that naturally, which is why the style suits it. The palette is narrow on purpose. Three or four tones, no more.

Two or three variations usually beat one perfect prompt. Same subject: "Simplified architectural 3D model of the Criciúma viewpoint tower, lit from inside at dusk, warm paper windows glowing". Or "Simplified architectural 3D model of the Criciúma viewpoint tower, half overgrown with paper vines and tiny paper leaves". Worth an attempt: "Simplified architectural 3D model of the Criciúma viewpoint tower, shown as an open cutaway with the rooms visible inside". Try "Simplified architectural 3D model of the Criciúma viewpoint tower, drawn as a flat architectural elevation on graph paper".

## Notes for the build

Score every fold line against a steel rule before creasing. 250 to 300 gsm card keeps the facets flat instead of bulging.
