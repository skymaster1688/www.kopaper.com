Describe "Gir", pick a style, and the papercraft generator does the rest — a pixel papercraft design, downloaded in seconds.

When the idea is abstract, contrast does the work: one bold colour against a quiet background. Chunky blocks and a cut-down palette give it the feel of a game sprite that was printed, trimmed and stood up by hand.

## Ways to take this further

One generation is a starting point, not a finished piece. If you would rather see it in context, try "Gir, shown in three different paper colourways side by side". If the first try feels too safe, try "Gir, shown small and large in the same frame for contrast". For a sharper take, try "Gir, built from kraft, white and tissue paper layers". For a moodier result, try "Gir, lit hard from one side so the paper shadow becomes part of the picture". Paste any of them into the generator and the same idea comes back with a different mood — same subject, different light, different level of detail.

## Building it in real paper

Work from one corner outwards so the grid squares stay aligned; drift shows up immediately on a pixel silhouette.

It is inspiration rather than a template: useful for proportions, colour and staging, not for tracing. Hunting for a place to start?
