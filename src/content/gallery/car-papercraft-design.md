"car" reads differently once it is folded. That is the short version of this cute vehicle papercraft design.

The cute style works by subtraction: fewer planes, rounder edges, and colour doing most of the emotional work. Vehicles depend on clean, straight cuts along the body panels — a steel ruler and a fresh blade make the difference between a toy that looks crisp and one that looks rough.

## Ways to take this further

The obvious version is easy; the interesting one usually takes a second prompt. If the first try feels too safe, try "car, parked on a strip of paper road with a few paper trees behind it". For a sharper take, try "car, leaning into a turn with paper motion streaks trailing behind". For a moodier result, try "car, presented on a blueprint-style sheet with visible fold marks". Want a warmer version? try "car, standing next to a tiny paper figure to show the scale". Paste any of them into the generator and the same idea comes back with a different mood — same subject, different light, different level of detail.

## Building it in real paper

For soft, rounded forms, crease gently over a dowel rather than pressing hard — the paper keeps its curve instead of kinking.
