One prompt, "Iguana", a few seconds of waiting. That gets you here.

Four-legged and winged subjects look best when the legs and joints are glued firmly. Heavier cardstock helps the figure stand on its own.

Facets are the whole idea. Each triangle catches light at a slightly different angle, so the geometry reads even in a flat image. The palette is narrow on purpose. Three or four tones, no more.

Same prompt, different staging, is usually enough. Last one: "Iguana, caught mid-leap with one paw lifted and a simple paper shadow beneath". Also worth generating: "Iguana, curled up asleep in a shallow paper nest under soft morning light". One more: "Iguana, mounted on a small paper plinth like a gallery piece". Another angle: "Iguana, as a pair of matching figures, one slightly smaller".

Cut every face before you fold any of them. Faceted builds go together much faster when the parts are already on the table.

You will not be able to print and fold this one. It is a picture of the finished idea.
