One line went into the prompt box. An animal in paper came out, styled as a low poly animal papercraft design.

Low poly means the shape is described by a small number of planes, which is exactly what folded paper does naturally. Animals live or die on the silhouette — get the ears, tail and stance right first, then add surface detail only if the shape already reads.

## Ways to take this further

Most people generate three or four versions before settling on one. For a moodier result, try "Iguana, caught mid-leap with one paw lifted and a simple paper shadow beneath". Want a warmer version? try "Iguana, curled up asleep in a shallow paper nest under soft morning light". To push the detail further, try "Iguana, mounted on a small paper plinth like a gallery piece". When you are ready for a variation, try "Iguana, as a pair of matching figures, one slightly smaller". Paste any of them into the generator and the same idea comes back with a different mood — same subject, different light, different level of detail.

## Building it in real paper

Stiffer card is your friend here — 260 gsm or above keeps large planes from flexing once the piece is standing.

Handy for deciding colour, scale and pose before you commit to card and blade. Still deciding on a direction?
