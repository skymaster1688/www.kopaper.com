Nothing was cut or glued to make the picture below. It came from the prompt "large rock in the foreground".

Let the edges stay visible. On abstract work, the cut line is often the most interesting part.

Low poly means the shape is described by a small number of planes. Folded paper does that naturally, which is why the style suits it.

The obvious version is not always the best one. One worth a go: "large rock in the foreground, shown in three different paper colourways side by side". A different direction: "large rock in the foreground, shown small and large in the same frame for contrast". Then there is "large rock in the foreground, built from kraft, white and tissue paper layers". Same subject: "large rock in the foreground, lit hard from one side so the paper shadow becomes part of the picture".

## Making it in paper

Score every fold line against a steel rule before creasing. 250 to 300 gsm card keeps the facets flat instead of bulging.
