Some ideas look better in paper than on paper. Rendered as a low poly animal papercraft design, "bat" finally looks like something you could pick up.

Facets are the whole idea here — each triangle catches the light at a slightly different angle, so the geometry reads even in a flat image. Four-legged and winged subjects look best when the legs and joints are glued firmly; a heavier cardstock helps the figure stand on its own.

## Ways to take this further

Most people generate three or four versions before settling on one. For a sharper take, try "bat, caught mid-leap with one paw lifted and a simple paper shadow beneath". For a moodier result, try "bat, curled up asleep in a shallow paper nest under soft morning light". Want a warmer version? try "bat, mounted on a small paper plinth like a gallery piece". To push the detail further, try "bat, as a pair of matching figures, one slightly smaller". Paste any of them into the generator and the same idea comes back with a different mood — same subject, different light, different level of detail.

## Building it in real paper

Stiffer card is your friend here — 260 gsm or above keeps large planes from flexing once the piece is standing.

Treat it as concept art for the real thing: it shows how the design should read, not where to cut. Looking for another angle?
