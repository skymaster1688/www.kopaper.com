"bat" is about as short as a brief gets. The result is a low poly animal papercraft design.

The look comes from flat triangular facets. A tight palette keeps every plane readable, and the hard edges do the modelling instead of shading. Flat lighting, soft falloff, no reflections. Paper is easier to read that way.

Same prompt, different staging, is usually enough. Try "bat, caught mid-leap with one paw lifted and a simple paper shadow beneath". Another variation: "bat, curled up asleep in a shallow paper nest under soft morning light". "bat, mounted on a small paper plinth like a gallery piece". Or this: "bat, as a pair of matching figures, one slightly smaller".

Cut every face before you fold any of them. Faceted builds go together much faster when the parts are already on the table.

This is a reference image rather than a template. The build is yours to work out.
