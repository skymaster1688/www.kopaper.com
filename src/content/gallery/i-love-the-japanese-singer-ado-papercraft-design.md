This started as "I love the Japanese singer ado". It ended up as a cute papercraft design.

Let the edges stay visible. On abstract work, the cut line is often the most interesting part.

Cute is mostly subtraction. Fewer planes, rounder edges, and colour doing the emotional work.

## Follow-up prompts

It is worth seeing the idea in more than one setting. Worth an attempt: "I love the Japanese singer ado, shown in three different paper colourways side by side". Try "I love the Japanese singer ado, shown small and large in the same frame for contrast". Another variation: "I love the Japanese singer ado, built from kraft, white and tissue paper layers". "I love the Japanese singer ado, lit hard from one side so the paper shadow becomes part of the picture".

## The paper part

For soft forms, crease gently over a dowel instead of pressing hard. The paper keeps its curve rather than kinking.
