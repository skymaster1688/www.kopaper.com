Half the charm of a paper model is the way it catches light. That is what this pixel papercraft design of "Invader zim" is leaning on.

Start with the largest pieces to set the silhouette, then fill in the smaller details last. Chunky blocks and a cut-down palette give it the feel of a game sprite that was printed, trimmed and stood up by hand.

## Ways to take this further

Most people generate three or four versions before settling on one. Want a warmer version? try "Invader zim, shown in three different paper colourways side by side". To push the detail further, try "Invader zim, shown small and large in the same frame for contrast". When you are ready for a variation, try "Invader zim, built from kraft, white and tissue paper layers". If you would rather see it in context, try "Invader zim, lit hard from one side so the paper shadow becomes part of the picture". Paste any of them into the generator and the same idea comes back with a different mood — same subject, different light, different level of detail.

## Building it in real paper

Build it on a grid: print a light 5 mm grid, count squares rather than eyeballing, and keep a fresh blade in the knife so every step stays square.
