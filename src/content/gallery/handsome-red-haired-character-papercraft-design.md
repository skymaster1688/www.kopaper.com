## How to turn this design into a real paper project

Pixel characters are a fantastic first figure to make from paper: their blocky shapes translate into simple rectangles and squares, so you get a clean result even with basic cuts.

### Materials you'll need
- Cardstock or light cardboard (200-250 gsm) for the figure
- A cutting mat, craft knife, and metal ruler
- White glue or a glue stick
- A pencil, ruler, and colored markers for details

### Step 1 — Simplify into blocks
Break the character into a head, torso, two arms, and two legs. Each becomes a flat rectangle or square panel. The pixel look actually helps here — every "pixel" is a natural guide for cutting.

### Step 2 — Cut the silhouette
Draw each part on cardstock and cut it out. For crisp edges, cut against a metal ruler with a craft knife; for kids, safety scissors work fine on thin cardstock.

### Step 3 — Add the face and details
Draw or glue on the face before assembly. Details are easier to add while the parts are flat — trying to draw on a standing figure is fiddly.

### Step 4 — Build the stand
For a standing figure, add a small base tab at the feet, or fold a simple cardstock stand behind the figure. A 3-5 cm tab folded at 90 degrees is enough to keep most figures upright.

### Troubleshooting
- **Figure keeps falling over** — widen the base tab or add a second tab at the back.
- **Details look messy** — use a thin marker and work on flat panels before gluing.
- **Paper won't hold a fold** — score the fold line with the back of a knife before bending.

## Ways to pose and display your paper character

A single standing figure is a great start, but a few extra touches turn it into a display piece:

- **Make a small scene** — place the character beside a paper building, tree, or vehicle for a diorama feel.
- **Add a ground line** — a thin cardstock strip under the feet grounds the figure and hides the stand.
- **Group a cast** — build two or three characters in matching style for a mini lineup on a shelf or desk.
- **Frame it flat** — pin the flat panels to a card and frame them as pixel-art wall decor; no stand needed.
- **Try different scales** — a small 8 cm version and a large 20 cm version of the same character look great side by side.

For a polished finish, use a cutting mat and a sharp craft knife for the silhouette, then color the edges with a matching marker so no white card edge shows. If you want the figure to face different directions, cut the head and body as separate panels and attach the head with a small paper fastener so it can turn.
