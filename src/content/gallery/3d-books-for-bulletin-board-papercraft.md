## How to turn this into a real paper project

A bulletin-board book display is one of the most satisfying papercraft builds: it looks complex, but it is mostly straight cuts, precise folds, and a little patience.

### Materials you'll need
- Cardstock or light cardboard (200-250 gsm works best for standing shapes)
- A cutting mat, craft knife, and metal ruler
- White glue or a glue stick
- A pencil and an eraser for marking fold lines

### Step 1 — Break the design into flat panels
Sketch the books as three simple rectangles (front cover, spine, pages). Each becomes one flat panel you can decorate before assembly — much easier than decorating after gluing.

### Step 2 — Score before you fold
Use the back of a craft knife (or a bone folder) to score along every fold line. Scored folds are straight, crisp, and won't tear the cardstock.

### Step 3 — Assemble inside out
Fold first, then glue. Apply glue to the tabs, press for a few seconds, and let each seam dry before moving on. Working inside-out keeps glue off the visible faces.

### Step 4 — Add depth with layers
Stack two or three cut-out "pages" per book to give the display a chunky, dimensional look. Layers are the fastest way to make flat paper read as 3D.

### Troubleshooting
- **Books won't stand** — add a small cardstock base or a hidden tab at the bottom edge.
- **Warped panels** — use less glue and press flat under a book while drying.
- **Rough edges** — sand lightly with fine sandpaper after cutting, or cover edges with matching paper tape.

## Ideas for your bulletin board display

These books shine in groups, and a bulletin board gives you room to play with layout:

- **Library corner** — stack the books next to a paper moon and a small potted plant for a cozy reading nook.
- **Classroom theme** — scatter the books with a giant yellow pencil and a paper apple for a back-to-school board.
- **Rainbow shelf** — line the books up in color order for a bright, orderly strip that reads at a distance.
- **Mini desk scene** — prop one book open on a paper desk with a tiny mug; it makes a charming focal point.
- **Seasonal swap** — change the cover colors and background to match the current season or holiday.

Keep the books at two or three sizes for depth, and attach them with removable adhesive so you can rearrange the board without tearing the paper. A few flat paper stars or leaves tucked between the books fill empty space without competing for attention.

## Frequently asked questions

**What paper should I use for bulletin-board books?** Cardstock (200-250 gsm) holds its shape and survives being pinned and repinned. **How big should the books be?** Start at about 10-15 cm tall — large enough to read from across the room, small enough to group several on one board. **Can I laminate them?** Yes, a quick lamination pass makes the books last through an entire school year.
