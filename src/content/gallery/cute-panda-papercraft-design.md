Paper is an odd medium for a machine to imitate, yet here it is: a cute animal papercraft design built around "cute panda".

The cute style works by subtraction: fewer planes, rounder edges, and colour doing most of the emotional work. If you build the paper version, give it a base: a small strip of card under the paws stops the whole figure from tipping forward.

## Ways to take this further

One generation is a starting point, not a finished piece. To push the detail further, try "cute panda, caught mid-leap with one paw lifted and a simple paper shadow beneath". When you are ready for a variation, try "cute panda, curled up asleep in a shallow paper nest under soft morning light". If you would rather see it in context, try "cute panda, mounted on a small paper plinth like a gallery piece". If the first try feels too safe, try "cute panda, as a pair of matching figures, one slightly smaller". Paste any of them into the generator and the same idea comes back with a different mood — same subject, different light, different level of detail.

## Building it in real paper

For soft, rounded forms, crease gently over a dowel rather than pressing hard — the paper keeps its curve instead of kinking.
