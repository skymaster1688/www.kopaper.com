Every papercrafter makes mistakes. The difference between a beginner and a pro isn't avoiding mistakes — it's knowing how to fix them. Whether you've cut a piece wrong, folded in the wrong direction, or ended up with a model that won't stand, most papercraft mistakes are fixable. Here's how to handle the most common ones.

## Cutting Mistakes

### Cut Outside the Line (Too Much Paper Removed)

**Problem:** You cut too far or at the wrong angle, removing more paper than intended.

**Fix:**
- **If the cut is on a tab (glue flap):** It doesn't matter — tabs are hidden inside the model. Continue as normal.
- **If the cut is on a visible edge:** Cut a thin strip of matching cardstock and glue it to the back, extending the edge. Trim to match.
- **If the piece is small and badly cut:** Reprint just that piece (if you have the template file) and recut.

**Prevention:** Cut slightly *inside* the line for visible edges — you can always trim more, but you can't add paper back. For tabs, cutting outside is fine.

### Cut a Fold Line (Dashed Line)

**Problem:** You accidentally cut along a dashed fold line instead of scoring it.

**Fix:**
- **If the cut is partial:** Score along the remaining uncut portion and fold carefully. The cut weakens the fold but the model will still hold together with glue.
- **If the cut goes all the way through:** The piece is now two pieces. Glue them back together with a small strip of cardstock on the back (unprinted side), then score and fold as normal. The repair will be hidden inside the model.

**Prevention:** Remember: solid lines = cut, dashed lines = fold. Before cutting, trace the line with your finger — if it's dashed, put the scissors down.

### Crooked / Wobbly Cuts

**Problem:** Your cuts aren't clean — they wobble or have jagged edges.

**Fix:**
- **For visible edges:** Trim the edge with small, careful cuts to straighten it. A craft knife and ruler give the straightest results.
- **For hidden edges (tabs):** No fix needed — they won't be visible.

**Prevention:**
- Use a craft knife with a fresh blade for straight cuts — scissors are better for curves.
- Cut with the piece flat on a cutting mat, not held in the air.
- For long straight cuts, use a steel ruler as a guide.

## Folding Mistakes

### Folded in the Wrong Direction

**Problem:** You folded a tab outward instead of inward, or a mountain fold as a valley fold.

**Fix:**
- **If the fold is recent and the crease is light:** Unfold and refold in the correct direction. Cardstock can handle one refold without weakening significantly.
- **If the crease is sharp and set:** Score the correct fold line on the opposite side, then fold. The wrong crease will be less visible but may still show. For visible surfaces, consider reprinting the piece.

**Prevention:** Look at the template carefully before folding. Tabs almost always fold inward (toward the unprinted side). Mountain folds fold away from you; valley folds fold toward you.

### Weak / Rounded Folds

**Problem:** Your folds are rounded instead of sharp, making the model look soft and unfinished.

**Fix:**
- **Unfold and rescore** the fold line with a bone folder, pressing firmly. Then refold and run the bone folder along the crease again.
- **If the paper is too thin:** The fold will never be sharp. Glue the piece to a heavier cardstock backing, then refold.

**Prevention:** Always score fold lines before folding. A bone folder gives the sharpest creases — the back of a spoon is a decent substitute but not as good.

## Glue Mistakes

### Too Much Glue (Warping / Stains)

**Problem:** You applied too much glue, and the paper warped or the glue soaked through to the printed side.

**Fix:**
- **For warped paper:** Place the piece under a heavy book (with wax paper between the book and the glue) while it dries. The weight flattens the warp.
- **For visible glue stains:** Let the glue dry completely, then gently scrape it off with your fingernail or a craft knife. If it has soaked into the paper, the stain is permanent — consider covering it with a small decorative element or reprinting.

**Prevention:** Apply a thin, even layer of glue with a toothpick or small brush. You want the tab coated, not drowning. Less is more.

### Glued the Wrong Pieces Together

**Problem:** You glued tab A to piece C instead of piece B.

**Fix:**
- **If the glue is still wet:** Gently pull the pieces apart. Wipe excess glue with a damp cloth. Let both pieces dry, then reglue correctly.
- **If the glue has dried:** Use a craft knife to carefully separate the joint — insert the blade between the glued surfaces and gently twist. This may tear the paper, so go slow. If the joint won't separate, cut the tab off and use a new strip of cardstock as a replacement tab.

**Prevention:** Dry-fit the pieces before applying glue. Hold them together without glue to confirm they match, then apply glue and assemble.

### Glue Won't Dry (Sticky Joints)

**Problem:** The glue stays sticky and the joint won't set.

**Fix:**
- **Humidity is the usual cause.** Use a fan or a hair dryer on cool setting to speed up drying.
- **If you're using a glue stick:** Some glue sticks never fully harden — they remain tacky. Switch to PVA glue for structural joints.

**Prevention:** Work in a well-ventilated area. Use PVA glue for joints that need to set hard.

## Structural Problems

### Model Won't Stand Up

**Problem:** The model tips over or can't support its own weight.

**Fix:**
- **Widen the base** — glue the model's feet to a wider cardstock base.
- **Add weight inside** — glue coins or a metal washer inside the base to lower the center of gravity.
- **Add internal supports** — glue cardstock strips inside the body as ribs to prevent squishing.
- **Use heavier cardstock** — if the model is floppy, 180+ gsm cardstock is needed.

For a complete guide, see our article on [how to make papercraft stand up](/learn/how-to-make-papercraft-stand-up/).

### Joints Coming Apart

**Problem:** Glued tabs separate when the model is handled.

**Fix:**
- **Reinforce from the inside** — if you can reach the joint, apply extra PVA glue along the seam from the inside.
- **Add a support strip** — glue a strip of cardstock across the joint on the inside for extra strength.
- **If the tab tore off:** Cut a new tab from scrap cardstock and glue it to the piece, then reattach.

**Prevention:** Use PVA glue (not glue stick) for structural joints. Press firmly for 10 seconds per joint. Let each major section dry before handling.

## Printing Problems

### Colors Wrong / Faded

**Problem:** The printed colors don't match the template, or they're faded.

**Fix:**
- **Check ink levels** — low ink causes faded colors and banding.
- **Calibrate your printer** — run a nozzle check and cleaning cycle.
- **Use the right paper settings** — select "cardstock" or "heavy paper" in your printer settings. Using "plain paper" settings on cardstock causes under-inking and faded colors.
- **Let ink dry** — inkjet ink can smudge if handled too soon. Wait 5 minutes before cutting.

### Printed at Wrong Scale (Tabs Don't Line Up)

**Problem:** The tabs don't match the corresponding pieces — everything is slightly too big or too small.

**Fix:**
- **Reprint at 100% scale.** The "fit to page" or "scale to fit" option resizes the template, which breaks tab alignment. Always select "actual size" or "100% scale."
- **If you've already cut the pieces:** You may be able to make them work with extra glue and careful alignment, but the seams will be visible. Reprinting is usually faster.

**Prevention:** Always check the print preview before printing. Confirm the scale is set to 100% and "fit to page" is disabled.

## When to Start Over

Some mistakes aren't worth fixing:
- The main body piece is torn beyond repair
- Multiple pieces are printed at the wrong scale
- The model is assembled incorrectly in a way that requires complete disassembly
- Ink has soaked through and ruined visible surfaces

In these cases, reprinting the template and starting fresh is faster and produces a better result. Keep the failed model as a learning reference — every mistake teaches you what to do differently next time.

Papercraft is forgiving. Most mistakes can be hidden, fixed, or worked around. The key is to stay calm, assess the damage, and choose the simplest fix.
