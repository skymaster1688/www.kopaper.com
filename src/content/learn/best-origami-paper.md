Picking the **best origami paper for beginners** is the single decision that most improves your early folds. The right sheet creases cleanly, holds its shape, and hides small mistakes. The wrong one fights you on every step. This guide compares the four papers you will meet most often — kami, tant, washi, and foil — by weight, size, price, and where each one shines, then gives a clear recommendation for a first pack.

## Why paper choice matters more than you think

Origami is pure geometry expressed through a single material. Unlike drawing, you cannot erase a wrong move; the paper remembers every crease. Thin paper takes many layers without bulging, while thicker paper gives structure to open, sculptural models. Beginners mostly need the former: a sheet that folds flat and stays put. That is why the global standard is a light, square sheet rather than the printer paper sitting in your tray.

## The four papers, compared

### Kami (standard origami paper)

- **Weight:** about 70–80 gsm — thin and easy.
- **Size:** usually 15 cm squares (also 7.5 cm and 24 cm).
- **Price:** cheap; a 100-sheet pack costs little.
- **Best for:** everyday folding, first models, classrooms.

Kami is the workhorse and the safest answer to "what should I buy first?" It is color-printed on one side and white on the back, which makes reverse folds easy to read. For the [best origami paper for beginners](/learn/best-origami-paper/), kami is the default recommendation.

### Tant

- **Weight:** about 80–90 gsm — slightly heavier.
- **Size:** 15 cm is common.
- **Price:** mid-range.
- **Best for:** models that need crisp, lasting edges and gift-quality finish.

Tant has a soft, matte feel and a solid color on both sides. It holds a sharper crease than kami, so it is excellent for animals and 3D shapes you want to display.

### Washi (traditional fiber paper)

- **Weight:** varies widely, often 50–100 gsm.
- **Size:** sold in squares and sheets.
- **Price:** higher; often sold by the sheet.
- **Best for:** wet folding and organic, sculptural forms.

True washi is made from long plant fibers, so it is strong even when thin and absorbs moisture for wet folding. It is beautiful but less uniform than machine-made paper — better saved for projects where texture matters.

### Foil (foil-backed paper)

- **Weight:** thin foil on a paper backing.
- **Size:** 15 cm and up.
- **Price:** mid to high.
- **Best for:** complex curves, shiny display models, experiments.

Foil paper keeps any shape you bend and looks striking, but it shows every crease and resists correction. Use it after you are comfortable; it is not the [best origami paper for beginners](/learn/best-origami-paper/) for daily practice.

## Weight, size, and price at a glance

| Paper | Weight (gsm) | Common size | Typical price | Start here? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kami | 70–80 | 15 cm | Low | ✅ Yes |
| Tant | 80–90 | 15 cm | Medium | Soon |
| Washi | 50–100 | mixed | High | Later |
| Foil | thin + backing | 15 cm | Medium | Later |

Notice that weight and size move together. Heavier paper in a small 7.5 cm square gets stiff fast; the same weight at 15 cm stays manageable. Stick to 15 cm while learning.

## How much to buy

A 100-sheet kami pack lasts a long time for a beginner — enough for dozens of cranes, boxes, and flowers with room to practice. Resist the urge to stock every type; depth with one good paper beats a shallow shelf of four.

## Caring for your paper

- Store sheets flat in a folder or box; curled paper folds unevenly.
- Keep them dry and away from direct sun, which fades colors.
- Separate colors you use often so you are not hunting mid-fold.
