If you have ever bought a pack of origami paper, you have probably noticed that most sheets are the same size: about 15 centimeters square. That is not an accident. The **origami paper size** we call standard exists because it is large enough to hold detail yet small enough to manage in two hands. This guide explains the common sizes, how they convert to inches, and how to cut your own squares from A4 or Letter stock.

## The Standard: 15 cm Square

The most common origami paper size is 15 x 15 cm (roughly 6 x 6 inches). Packs of this size are sold in nearly every craft store and are the default for most books and tutorials. A 15 cm square is comfortable to fold, shows creases clearly, and produces a finished model that fits in your palm.

In Japan, where much of the modern folding vocabulary developed, the traditional size is washi sheets around 15 cm. The habit stuck, and today 15 cm is the de facto international standard for practice and display.

## Smaller and Larger Common Sizes

Not every model wants the same square.

### 7.5 cm (About 3 Inches)

Half the standard size, used for tiny, detailed models such as miniature animals or modular units where you need many pieces. The smaller square keeps bulk down so layered tips stay sharp.

### 24 cm to 30 cm (About 9 to 12 Inches)

Larger squares suit big display pieces, wet folding, and models with many steps where small paper becomes fiddly. They are also friendly for teaching groups, since the folds are easy to see from a distance.

### Odd Sizes for Specific Models

Some designs call for rectangles (like the 1:2 ratio for certain boxes and envelopes) or long strips for chains. But when a project just says "a square of paper," assume 15 cm unless stated otherwise.

## Converting to Inches

If you buy paper by the inch or follow tutorials from the United States, use these rough equivalents:

- 7.5 cm ≈ 3 inches
- 15 cm ≈ 5.9 inches (commonly called 6 inches)
- 24 cm ≈ 9.4 inches
- 30 cm ≈ 11.8 inches (close to 12 inches)

Most "6 inch origami paper" sold online is actually 15 cm. The labels are used interchangeably, and either works for the same models.

## Why Square Matters More Than Exact Size

Almost every traditional model is designed around a square, not a specific measurement. That means a 14 cm square and a 16 cm square will both fold the same crane. The key is that all four sides are equal and all four corners are right angles. You can scale most designs up or down freely; the proportions stay intact.

So if you cannot find exact 15 cm paper, do not worry. Any clean square will do. The standard size is a convenience, not a rule.

## Cutting Your Own Squares from A4

Plain printer paper is rectangular, but you can turn it into origami ready squares in under a minute. This is especially handy when you want to practice without opening a fresh pack. Our guide on [using printer paper for origami](/learn/printer-paper-for-origami/) covers when that material is a good fit.

To cut one square from an A4 sheet (210 x 297 mm):

1. Place the sheet flat in portrait orientation.
2. Fold the top left corner down so the top edge meets the right edge. Crease firmly.
3. The fold forms a right triangle at the top, with a rectangular strip below it.
4. Cut or tear along the bottom edge of that triangle.
5. Unfold the triangle. You now have a 210 x 210 mm square.

That square is larger than standard, which is fine for practice. If you want something closer to 15 cm, start from A5 (half of A4) instead, or trim the 210 mm square down.

## Cutting from Letter Paper

US Letter paper is 8.5 x 11 inches. The same fold and cut method works: fold one short edge to the long edge, crease, trim the excess, and unfold into a square. The result is an 8.5 inch square, about 21.6 cm, slightly larger than standard but perfectly usable.

## Choosing a Size for Your Project

A simple way to decide:

- **Learning a new fold** → standard 15 cm, or larger if your hands are still finding the moves.
- **Tiny detailed animal** → 7.5 cm so layers stay thin.
- **Big display piece** → 24 to 30 cm for presence and visibility.
- **Classroom or kids** → whatever square you can cut quickly; size is not critical.

If you are unsure, start at 15 cm. It is the standard for good reason.
