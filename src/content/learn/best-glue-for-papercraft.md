Choosing the **best glue for papercraft** matters more than most beginners realize. The wrong glue warps your cardstock, leaves visible stains, or — worse — fails to hold the model together. The right glue disappears into the seam, dries clear, and holds strong for years.

## Quick answer: which glue for which job

If you are here for a single recommendation, this table covers ninety percent of papercraft joints.

| Job | Best choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main body and structural seams | PVA white glue | Strongest paper-to-paper bond, dries clear |
| Small tabs, thin edges, tight corners | Glue stick | No squeeze-out, no warp, fast tack |
| Flat panels, card bases, box lids | Double-sided tape | Zero moisture, perfectly flat result |
| Low poly models with visible facets | PVA applied with a toothpick | Invisible seam, and the joint stays rigid |
| Thin or lightweight paper (under 120 gsm) | Glue stick, or PVA sparingly | PVA's water content curls thin sheets |
| Vertical surfaces and overhead tabs | Tacky glue | Thicker body, does not run |
| Kids and first models | Glue stick | Forgiving, no drying time, no stains |

**The practical answer for most people:** one bottle of PVA, one glue stick, and a roll of double-sided tape. Use all three in the same model — PVA for the frame, stick for small tabs, tape for anything that has to stay flat.

## Drying time at a glance

| Glue | Grab / tack | Fully set | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Glue stick | 5–10 seconds | 1–2 minutes | Fastest to handle, weakest under load |
| PVA white glue | 1–5 minutes | 30–60 minutes | 24 hours for full strength on heavy joints |
| Tacky glue | 1–2 minutes | 20–40 minutes | Holds vertical tabs without sliding |
| Double-sided tape | Instant | Instant | No cure time, but no repositioning either |
| Rubber cement | Instant | 10 minutes | Repositionable before it sets; not permanent long-term |

Plan assembly so you are never waiting on a wet joint. Build sub-assemblies, set them aside, and rotate through them — by the time you return to the first one it will hold.

## Types of Glue for Papercraft

### PVA Glue (White Glue)

PVA (polyvinyl acetate) glue is the workhorse of papercraft. It's the white glue you probably used in school — think Elmer's or similar brands.

**Best for:** Structural bonds, large tabs, base assembly, and any joint that needs strength.

**Pros:**
- Very strong when dry
- Dries clear (most brands)
- Affordable and widely available
- Reusable if you work quickly

**Cons:**
- Can warp thin paper if applied too thickly
- Takes 5–15 minutes to set fully
- Can squeeze out from seams if over-applied

**Technique:** Apply a thin layer with a toothpick or a small brush. Less is more — you want the tab coated, not drowning.

### Glue Sticks

Glue sticks are convenient and mess-free. They work best for quick, low-stress joints.

**Best for:** Small tabs, flat areas, and quick assembly where you don't need maximum strength.

**Pros:**
- No mess, no warping
- Dries instantly (tack)
- Easy to control
- Great for kids and beginners

**Cons:**
- Not as strong as PVA for structural joints
- Can fail under stress (heavy models, standing figures)
- Some brands go on purple and dry clear — check the label

**Technique:** Apply evenly to the entire tab surface. Press firmly for 5 seconds. For structural parts, reinforce with a dot of PVA on the inside.

### Double-Sided Tape

Double-sided tape is the cleanest option — no drying time, no warping, no mess.

**Best for:** Flat panels, card bases, and any joint where you want zero visible glue.

**Pros:**
- Instant bond
- No warping or staining
- Very clean finish
- Great for flat papercraft and cards

**Cons:**
- Hard to reposition once applied
- Not ideal for curved or 3D joints
- Can be expensive for large projects
- Thicker tapes create visible gaps

**Technique:** Apply the tape to the tab before folding. Peel the backing and press firmly. Work carefully — you only get one chance.

### Specialty Glues

- **Tacky glue** — thicker than PVA, great for vertical surfaces where you don't want glue to run.
- **Rubber cement** — repositionable, but not permanent; good for mockups, not finished models.
- **Super glue (cyanoacrylate)** — bonds instantly, but can melt some papers and is overkill for cardstock. Avoid unless you're working with very heavy materials.

## Which Glue Should You Use?

| Situation | Recommended Glue |
|---|---|
| Main body assembly | PVA glue |
| Small tabs and details | Glue stick |
| Flat panels and bases | Double-sided tape |
| Heavy structural parts (dragons, buildings) | PVA glue (thick layer) |
| Kids/beginners | Glue stick |
| Clean, no-mess finish | Double-sided tape |

**Most papercrafters use a combination:** PVA for the main structure, glue stick for small tabs, and double-sided tape for flat panels.

## PVA vs glue stick: the one comparison that matters

Most "which glue?" questions come down to this pair. They are not rivals — they solve different problems.

| | PVA white glue | Glue stick |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bond strength on cardstock | Very high; the paper usually tears before the joint fails | Moderate; holds flat tabs, fails on stressed corners |
| Moisture | Water-based, so it can curl thin paper | Low moisture, minimal warp |
| Squeeze-out | Yes, if over-applied — clean it immediately | Almost none |
| Control | Needs a toothpick or brush for small work | Direct, no tool needed |
| Cost per model | Very low | Low |
| Best at | Anything load-bearing | Anything fiddly |

A useful rule: **if the joint has to hold weight or tension, use PVA. If the joint only has to stay aligned while you move on, use a glue stick.** Models that stand on legs, wings that cantilever, and any base plate are all PVA territory.

## Best glue for low poly paper models

Low poly models hide nothing. There are no rounded surfaces to absorb a mistake, and every facet meets its neighbour at a hard angle, so a blob of glue at that edge is immediately visible. The seam has to be invisible *and* rigid — a joint that creeps even slightly will throw the next facet out of alignment, and the error compounds across the model.

What works:

1. **PVA through a fine tip.** Decant a little into a small squeeze bottle with a metal tip, or dip a toothpick. You want a bead about the width of the paper's thickness along the inside of the tab — nothing more.
2. **Glue the tab, not the face.** Applying to the printed face guarantees squeeze-out on the visible side. Put the bead on the tab, fold it inside, hold for ten seconds.
3. **Score before you glue.** A crisp fold line means the tab sits flat against the inside face; a soft fold means a gap that needs filler glue, which is what warps the panel.

For a faceted subject like a <a href="/gallery/dragon/">low poly dragon</a>, assemble in sub-sections — torso, then wings, then head — and let each one cure fully before joining. Wet joints plus handling is the main cause of a model that will not sit square.

## Best glue for thin paper

Thin paper (under about 120 gsm) behaves differently from cardstock: it absorbs water fast, cockles along the glued edge, and shows every wrinkle once dry. The problem is not the glue's strength — it is its water content.

Three fixes, in order of preference:

- **Use a glue stick.** Lowest moisture of the common options and enough hold for folded seams in light paper.
- **Use double-sided tape** for anything flat. No moisture at all, so nothing can curl.
- **If you need PVA**, apply it to one side only, press the joint, then immediately weight it under a book for ten minutes. The weight is doing as much work as the glue.

Never thin PVA with water for light paper. It lowers the solids content, extends drying time, and makes cockling more likely, not less.

## How to glue paper without wrinkles

Wrinkles are a moisture problem, almost always caused by too much glue rather than the wrong kind.

- **Less than you think.** A tab needs a film, not a bead. If glue squeezes out when you press, you used roughly twice what was needed.
- **Spread it.** A toothpick or small brush turns a blob into an even film, which dries flat instead of pooling.
- **Weight flat parts.** Flat panels glued with PVA should sit under a book or a sheet of glass while curing. Ten minutes is enough to prevent most curl.
- **Do not re-wet a dried wrinkle.** Adding water to relax a wrinkle usually makes it worse. Cut the panel and redo it, or hide the seam.
- **Match the stock to the joint.** Heavier cardstock resists moisture far better; our guide on <a href="/learn/what-paper-for-origami/">what paper to use</a> explains which weights suit folded versus glued work.

## Glue Application Tips

1. **Use a toothpick** for precise PVA application — the bottle nozzle applies too much.
2. **Wipe excess glue immediately** with a damp paper towel — dried glue is harder to remove.
3. **Let joints dry** for 5 minutes before handling the model.
4. **Work in sections** — glue one area, let it set, then move to the next.
5. **Store PVA glue upside down** to prevent the nozzle from drying out.

## Common Glue Problems

- **Paper warping** — you used too much glue. Apply a thinner layer next time.
- **Visible glue stains** — glue squeezed out from the seam. Wipe immediately with a damp cloth.
- **Joints falling apart** — glue stick isn't strong enough for that part. Reinforce with PVA on the inside.
- **Glue won't dry** — you're in a humid environment. Use a fan or a dehumidifier.
