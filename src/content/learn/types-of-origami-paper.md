If you have folded with more than one kind of sheet, you already know that **types of origami paper** are not interchangeable. Each one creases, stretches, and shows its color differently, which quietly decides what models you can make. This glossary walks through the four papers you will meet most often — kami, tant, washi, and foil — describing how each one looks and behaves, with the projects where it shines. Read it alongside our [best origami paper for beginners](/learn/best-origami-paper/) guide and our [what paper to use for origami](/learn/what-paper-for-origami/) breakdown.

## Kami — the everyday standard

Kami is the paper most people picture when they hear "origami paper." It is a thin, machine-made sheet, usually about 70–80 gsm, printed in a solid color on the front and left white on the back.

- **Look:** matte, even color, one colored face and one white face.
- **Feel:** light, a little crisp, easy to crease.
- **Folds:** takes sharp mountain and valley folds without resistance; the two-tone face makes reverse folds easy to read.
- **Best for:** daily practice, first models, classroom folding, and almost anything in our [origami tutorials](/origami/).

Because it is cheap and forgiving, kami is the default answer to most "which paper?" questions. If you want a decorative piece, kami is also the base material for many [printable paper craft templates](/templates/) you can color before folding.

## Tant — the crisp upgrade

Tant is slightly heavier than kami, around 80–90 gsm, and is dyed the same color on both sides. It has a soft, almost velvety matte surface.

- **Look:** solid, even color front and back, no white reverse.
- **Feel:** sturdy yet still foldable.
- **Folds:** holds a sharper, longer-lasting crease than kami; resists springing open on complex steps.
- **Best for:** animals, display pieces, and models where a white back would look unfinished.

Tant is the natural next step once kami feels too light. It is the paper we recommend for a [folded dragon](/templates/dragon/) you plan to keep on a shelf, because the heavier sheet keeps the sculpted shape.

## Washi — the traditional fiber paper

Washi is not a single product but a family of papers made from long plant fibers (like mulberry bark). Its weight varies widely, often 50–100 gsm, and it is usually sold by the sheet rather than in huge packs.

- **Look:** natural, slightly textured surface; colors can be uneven and organic.
- **Feel:** strong for its thinness, absorbs a little moisture.
- **Folds:** excellent for wet folding, where you dampen the sheet to bend soft, rounded curves.
- **Best for:** organic, sculptural forms — flowers, animals, and soft abstract shapes.

Washi is the paper behind a beautiful [folded flower](/origami/flower/), where the texture reads as "real" even up close. It is less uniform than machine paper, so beginners may prefer to start elsewhere.

## Foil — the shiny experiment

Foil paper is a thin sheet of metallic film laminated onto a paper backing. It is flexible in a different way from the others: it holds any shape you bend it into.

- **Look:** glossy, reflective, often in bright metallics or saturated colors.
- **Feel:** smooth and springy; the backing gives it body.
- **Folds:** keeps curved and rounded forms perfectly, but every crease shows permanently.
- **Best for:** complex curves, shiny display models, and design experiments.

Foil rewards confidence and punishes mistakes — a wrong crease cannot be un-creased. Save it for projects where the shine is the point.

## How to choose at a glance

| Paper | Two-tone? | Weight | Holds shape | Start here? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kami | Yes (colored/white) | Light | Good | ✅ First |
| Tant | No (both sides) | Medium | Very good | Soon |
| Washi | Varies | Light–medium | Good (wet fold) | Later |
| Foil | N/A (shiny) | Thin + backing | Perfect | Specials |

The pattern is simple: lighter papers teach you the basics, heavier and specialty papers let you finish a piece you are proud to display.

## Matching paper to your project

- **Learning a new fold** → kami, every time.
- **A gift or shelf model** → tant for clean edges, washi for organic texture.
- **A shiny showpiece** → foil, once the folds are comfortable.
